Nearly everyone has encountered the typical issue of a bathtub drain clogged with hair. Whether you have long locks or typical shedding, hair accumulation in the drain can soon cause problems. Not only is it inconvenient—standing in a few inches of water as you shower—but it can cause more major plumbing problems if neglected. Maintaining a bathroom in good working order depends on an awareness of why this occurs and how to manage it. This blog will go over doable techniques to clear a bathtub drain clogged with hair, preventative steps to stop blockages in the future, and when it might be time to call in a professional.

## Why Does Hair Clog the Bathtub Drain?

One common cause of clogged drains is hair. As hair naturally falls out and washes down the drain, it can accumulate and form a dense, fibrous mass. Hair tends to tangle with soap scum, dirt, and other debris, creating a blockage that impedes water flow. What starts as a minor inconvenience can quickly escalate if not addressed, leading to a complete blockage. This not only causes water to back up into your bathtub but can also impact other parts of your plumbing system. Regular drain cleaning and maintenance are key to preventing such issues.

## Methods to Unclog a Bathtub Drain Clogged with Hair

If you’re dealing with a bathtub clogged with hair, several effective methods can help restore proper drainage. Whether you plan to tackle the problem yourself or are exploring options before seeking professional help, these techniques can be effective:

Manual Removal:

- Tools Needed: Gloves, flashlight, and a drain snake or wire hanger. Procedure: Start by wearing gloves and using a flashlight to inspect the drain. Carefully reach into the drain with your fingers or use a tool like a drain snake or wire hanger to pull out visible hair. A drain snake is particularly useful for deeper blockages. Insert the snake into the drain, twist, and carefully pull it out to remove the hair and any other debris.

Use a Plunger:

- Tools Needed: Plunger. Procedure: Place the plunger over the drain to create a seal. Add a small amount of water to the tub to ensure the plunger covers the drain completely. Use the plunger with firm, rapid up-and-down motions to generate suction and dislodge the blockage. This method can help push the hair through the drain and restore normal water flow

Baking Soda and Vinegar:

- Ingredients Needed: Baking soda, vinegar, and hot water. Procedure: Pour half a cup of baking soda down the drain, followed by half a cup of vinegar. Allow the mixture to fizz and bubble for 15 to 20 minutes, which helps break down the hair and other debris. Afterward, flush the drain with hot water to clear away any remaining residue and to ensure the blockage is fully cleared.

Chemical Drain Cleaners:

- Tools Needed: Commercial drain cleaner. Procedure: Follow the manufacturer’s instructions carefully to avoid damaging your pipes. These products are generally intended as a last resort if other methods fail to clear the clog. Overuse or improper application can lead to pipe damage or worsening of the clog. Chemical drain cleaners can be effective but should be used with caution.

## How to Prevent Hair from Clogging the Bathtub Drain

Preventing hair clogs is simpler than dealing with them. Here are some proactive measures:

- Install a Drain Cover: A drain cover or hair catcher is an affordable and effective tool that prevents hair from entering your plumbing system. These devices sit over the drain and trap hair and other debris, which can be easily cleaned out.

- Regular Maintenance: Even with a drain cover, it’s important to perform regular maintenance. Pouring boiling water down the drain once a week can help dissolve soap scum and other minor accumulations before they become problematic.

- Brush Before Showering: Brushing your hair before showering can significantly reduce the amount of hair that ends up in the drain. Trapped hair also feeds odor-causing bacteria, which is a common reason behind odors in shower drains. This simple step helps minimize the risk of clogs and keeps your plumbing system running smoothly.

While many hair clogs can be managed with the methods described above, there are situations where professional help is necessary. If you’ve tried these techniques and the clog persists, or if you notice other plumbing issues such as persistent slow drainage or water backing up into other fixtures, it may be time to contact a professional plumber. An experienced plumber can provide a thorough inspection, use advanced tools to clear the blockage, and address any underlying issues that may be affecting your plumbing system.
