Have you opened your monthly bill and gasped? If so, ask yourself: Can a running toilet increase water bill? The answer is a resounding yes. This is generally the first indication that something is wrong.

If the water level in the tank fluctuates erratically, it indicates that water is leaking somewhere.

Repeated extended refills indicate that the fill valve is faulty or that the float level is too low.

## How to Fix a Running Toilet

If you’re handy, you might be able to fix it without contacting a plumber.

## Adjusting or Replacing the Flapper Valve

Check that the flapper seals correctly. If not, try tweaking it or replacing it entirely.

## Replacing the Fill Valve

If the fault is with the fill valve, switch off the water supply and replace it. It is available at any hardware shop.

## Fixing a Misaligned Chain

A simple chain adjustment might prevent your toilet constantly running. Make sure it is neither too tight nor too loose.

## Cleaning the Overflow Tube

Debris or accumulation in the overflow tube might disrupt the water flow. Clean it up with a pipe cleaner or a soft brush.

## Checking the Toilet Tank for Leaks

Add food coloring to the tank’s water and wait 30 minutes. If the color reflects in the bowl, it indicates a leak.

## Adjusting the Toilet Tank Water Level

Ensure that the float is adjusted to maintain the proper water level. Too much water might cause overflow, while too little can result in ineffective flushing.

## Replacing a Faulty Fill Tube

The fill tube connects to the overflow pipe. If it is disconnected or broken, replace it to resolve water flow issues.

## Tightening Loose Toilet Parts

Sometimes, all it takes is tightening a few screws or nuts inside the tank to stop the constant refill cycle.
