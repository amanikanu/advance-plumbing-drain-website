Nothing ruins a hot shower faster than a sulfur smell wafting from the faucet. When your water heater smells like rotten eggs, it’s natural to worry something is seriously wrong. A plumber can identify the cause and explain whether a repair will fix it or whether you should consider installing a new water heater in San Francisco.

## What Causes a Rotten Egg Smell in Hot Water?

Hydrogen sulfide gas causes the rotten egg smell in hot water. It may form when sulfate-reducing bacteria grow inside the tank or when sulfates in the water react with the anode rod. The odor rarely means the tank is about to fail.

These bacteria thrive in warm, low-oxygen environments, including water stored between 100°F and 130°F.

## How Does the Anode Rod Affect the Smell?

Most tank-style water heaters use a magnesium or aluminum anode rod to protect the steel tank from corrosion. The rod gradually breaks down, so the tank doesn’t rust as quickly. However, certain rod materials can contribute to hydrogen sulfide production, causing or worsening the rotten egg smell.

## Does the Type of Rod Matter?

A magnesium anode can support the chemical conversion of sulfates into hydrogen sulfide, contributing to the odor. An aluminum-zinc rod, on the other hand, often reduces the smell because it is less likely to promote hydrogen sulfide production, though a plumber should confirm the best choice for your water.

A depleted rod can leave the tank vulnerable to rust, so a plumber should inspect the anode rod and tank for corrosion.

Run the cold water first and smell it. If only the hot water smells, the tank is likely the source. If both hot and cold water smell, the source may be the water supply or another part of the plumbing system, such as the water softener.

## Ways To Address the Smell

Possible professional treatment options include:

- Flush the tank: Regular water heater maintenance and flushing remove sediment where bacteria may collect.

- Inspect the anode rod: A plumber can determine whether a manufacturer-approved alternative is suitable.

- Disinfect the tank: A plumber may use an approved chlorine or hydrogen peroxide treatment.

Do not raise the temperature or add disinfectants yourself. Hot water can cause severe scalds, and improper treatment may damage the system.

Watch for these warning signs alongside the smell:

- Rusty water: Brown or orange hot water often points to a corroding tank.

- Temperature problems: Sediment buildup or a faulty component may cause temperature changes or leave you with no hot water in your home.

If you notice these signs or the smell returns after treatment, contact a licensed plumber to inspect the system and recommend a plan of action.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Odors

These answers explain what causes sulfur odors and how treatment works. You will also learn when to contact a plumber.

## Is It Safe To Shower With Water That Smells Like Rotten Eggs?

Sulfur bacteria are generally harmless, but odor alone cannot confirm water safety. Have the supply evaluated if both hot and cold water smell.

## How Do I Get Rid of the Rotten Egg Smell in My Water Heater?

The right fix depends on the source. A plumber may flush or disinfect the tank and determine whether to replace the anode.

## Why Does Only My Hot Water Smell Like Sulfur?

Hot water may smell like sulfur when bacteria inside the tank or a reaction involving the anode rod produces hydrogen sulfide. Cold water does not pass through the heater.

## How Often Should I Flush My Water Heater?

A water heater should be flushed about once a year, or twice a year in homes with hard water.
