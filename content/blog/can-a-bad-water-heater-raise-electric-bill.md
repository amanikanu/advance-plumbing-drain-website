A water heater problem does not always start with a leak. Sometimes, the first sign is less hot water during normal use. When that happens, you may wonder, “Can a bad water heater raise electric bill costs?”

## Can a Bad Water Heater Raise an Electric Bill?

Yes, a failing water heater can raise your electric bill. Different types of water heaters can waste energy when parts wear down, or buildup affects performance. The points below explain why energy costs may increase:

- Poor insulation in tank models can waste energy while stored water stays hot.

- Faulty electric elements or gas components can reduce heating performance.

- Sediment buildup can accumulate in tank systems and reduce heating efficiency.

Watch for these common signs that your unit is wasting energy:

- Your electric bill increases without a noticeable change in hot water use.

- Warm water takes longer to reach fixtures that previously heated quickly.

- Hot water is running out faster than it did before.

- Banging or rumbling sounds are coming from the tank, which often indicate a noisy water heater struggling with heavy sediment scale that forces components to overheat.

## What Determines the Efficiency of a Tankless Water Heater?

Tankless water heater efficiency depends on how well the unit matches household demand. If the system is undersized, it may struggle during showers or laundry use. Flow rate also affects performance when multiple fixtures run simultaneously. Water quality can change how well heat transfers through the unit, which becomes especially critical after installing water heater systems to prevent premature efficiency losses. Hard water may leave mineral deposits inside narrow passages.

## Water Heater Maintenance To Improve Efficiency

Poor upkeep can cause the system to run longer than necessary. That connection explains why “Can a bad water heater raise electric bill costs?” remains a common concern among homeowners. Flushing tank systems or descaling tankless units helps remove buildup, and checking connections minimizes the risk of a leak from drain valve caused by mineral corrosion. Checking temperature settings also helps limit excessive electricity use.

## Does an Old Water Heater Use More Electricity?

Yes, older units lose efficiency as parts wear and sediment collects. After 10 to 15 years, many tank water heaters work less efficiently.

That depends on the age and condition of your unit. A repair makes sense for newer systems, while a replacement is the smarter call for older ones with recurring issues.

## How Much Electricity Does a Water Heater Use?

Water heating accounts for about 18% of home energy use. The exact amount depends on usage and system condition. Tank size also affects energy demand.
