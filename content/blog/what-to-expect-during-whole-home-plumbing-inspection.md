Plumbing issues are not always limited to the fixture where symptoms first appear. Because supply and drainage lines connect different parts of a home, one problem may point to conditions elsewhere in the system.

## How Does a Whole-Home Plumbing Inspection Begin?

The visit usually starts with a brief discussion about recent plumbing concerns. A technician may ask about recurring clogs or changes in performance so those areas can receive closer attention during the inspection.

The plumber then works through the property methodically, checking accessible components in each area. This approach provides a clearer picture of how the plumbing system is performing overall.

## What Does a Plumber Check During the Inspection?

A whole-home inspection reviews accessible plumbing components and how key parts of the system are performing. This covers:

- Plumbing fixtures: Faucets, sinks, and toilets are checked for leaks or worn parts.

- Shut-off valves: Accessible valves are tested for proper operation.

- Exposed piping: Visible pipes are examined for corrosion or damage.

- Connections: Accessible joints are checked for moisture or deterioration.

## How Is Water Pressure Tested?

A plumber can measure water pressure using a gauge connected to an appropriate fixture or hose connection. The reading shows whether pressure entering and moving through the home is within an acceptable range according to the International Plumbing Code, which sets a maximum limit of 80 PSI.

High pressure can place added strain on plumbing components. Low pressure may indicate a supply problem or another issue that requires investigation. Testing is especially useful when weak flow affects several fixtures rather than one isolated faucet.

## How Are Hidden Pipe Leaks Checked?

If the source remains unclear, additional leak-detection methods may be recommended. Homeowners can also review these signs of a major plumbing problem for other changes that deserve attention.

## What Happens During a Drain Line Check?

Drainage is evaluated separately from the water-supply side of the system. A technician may run water through fixtures to observe how quickly it clears.

If a recurring problem suggests a deeper blockage or damaged line, a camera inspection may be recommended. A small camera can examine drain lines internally without opening walls or floors. Drain cleaning may be suggested if the inspection confirms an obstruction that needs removal.

## How Is the Water Heater Reviewed?

The water heater is checked for visible leakage or corrosion that could affect performance. The technician will also review its accessible connections and safety components. If the unit shows signs of sediment buildup or another maintenance concern, the plumber can explain whether additional service is appropriate.

A whole-home plumbing inspection makes sense at these key transition points:

- Before buying a home: An inspection can reveal plumbing concerns before you take ownership.

- Before a major remodel: Checking the system first can help prevent surprises once work begins.

- After a long vacancy: Unused plumbing may need review before regular use resumes.

- Before replacing major fixtures: An inspection can confirm whether surrounding plumbing is ready for the upgrade.

Planning the inspection around these events can give homeowners useful information before they make larger decisions about the property.

## Frequently Asked Questions

These common questions can help homeowners prepare for an inspection.

## How Long Does a Whole-Home Plumbing Inspection Take?

The length of the visit depends on the size of the home and how much of the plumbing is accessible.

## How Often Should I Have My Plumbing Inspected?

Inspection frequency depends on the age and condition of the system. A plumber can recommend an interval after evaluating the property.

## How Should I Prepare for the Inspection?

Clear access around sinks and other plumbing fixtures before the technician arrives. It is also helpful to make note of any recent plumbing changes you’ve noticed.
