A gas odor or hissing sound near a water heater is a reason to leave the area, not to investigate the appliance. Get everyone outside. Do not use a phone, operate electrical switches, light a flame, or try to adjust the heater while inside.

## Call from a safe location

Once you are safely away from the building, call your gas utility’s emergency line or 911. Follow their instructions. Do not re-enter until emergency personnel or the utility say it is safe.

Do not delay evacuation to open windows, turn valves, look for the source, or test fittings. A service request form is not an emergency reporting channel.

## When can the water heater be inspected?

After the property has been cleared, a qualified professional can inspect the appliance and its connections. Do not attempt to repair, restart, or relight a heater associated with a suspected gas leak.

## Natural gas and carbon monoxide are different hazards

A standard carbon monoxide alarm does not detect a natural gas leak. Carbon monoxide is odorless and can be produced by incomplete combustion. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, move everyone outside and contact emergency services from a safe location.
