Water heater size tracks your household size more closely than anything else, since every shower and appliance cycle pulls hot water from one tank. Hot water demand varies based on how your household uses it, so household size is only a starting point.

A soaking tub or back-to-back showers push you past what the label promises. The real answer to “What size water heater do I need?” starts with counting people, not gallons.

Peak-hour demand is the amount of hot water your home uses during its busiest one-hour period. Estimate your hot-water demand during that hour, then compare it with the unit’s first-hour rating.

The following ranges provide a starting point:

- One to two people: A 30- to 40-gallon tank should be enough.

- Three to four people: Plan on 40 to 50 gallons.

- Five or more people: Look at 60- to 80-gallon models.

Fuel type shifts those ranges, since gas reheats faster than electric among water heater types.

Replacing a 40-gallon unit with an 80-gallon model may require changes to the venting, connections, or other installation components to meet applicable IPC and San Francisco Plumbing Code requirements. Listen for a hiss at the temperature and pressure relief valve, and watch for rust-tinted water.

Improper installation can affect warranty coverage, so leaving it to an expert can help ensure the manufacturer’s installation requirements and local code are followed. If you’re still unsure what size water heater is right for your home, a licensed plumber can run the calculation. After installation, following standard post-installation steps helps verify temperature settings and check for initial leaks.

## Water Heater Sizing: Common Questions

Here are answers to some of the key things homeowners consider when choosing a water heater.

## Is a Bigger Water Heater Always Better?

A bigger water heater is not always better. Oversized tanks reheat water nobody uses, raising standby heat loss.

## How Much Hot Water Does One Shower Use?

One shower uses about 10 to 20 gallons. WaterSense showerheads use no more than two gallons per minute, reducing hot-water use.

## Does Sizing Work Differently for Tankless Units?

Tankless units are sized by flow rate and temperature rise, not tank capacity.
